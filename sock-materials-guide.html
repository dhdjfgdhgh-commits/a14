Sock Materials Guide

The fibre on the label decides how warm, dry, soft and long-lasting a sock will be. Here is how to read it.

1

Natural fibres

Merino wool is fine, soft wool that insulates even when damp and resists odour. Cotton is soft and breathable but slow to dry. Bamboo viscose feels silky and cool, but is less hard-wearing.

2

Synthetic fibres

Nylon (polyamide) brings strength; polyester moves sweat and dries fast; elastane gives stretch and recovery. On their own they can feel clammy; in a blend they make socks last.

3

Read a blend

70% merino · 28% nylon · 2% elastaneA classic hiking blend: warm, tough and stays put.
80% cotton · 18% polyamide · 2% elastaneAn everyday sock that holds its shape.
90% polyester · 10% elastaneA running or gym sock built to dry fast.
4

Thickness and knit

Thin socks suit dress shoes and close-fitting trainers. Cushioned socks use terry loops inside, often only at the heel and ball. Full-cushion socks are warmest and need roomier boots.

5

Care by fibre

  • Wool: wash cool on a wool or gentle cycle, dry flat or on a rack.
  • Cotton blends: 30–40°C, inside out.
  • Synthetics: skip fabric softener, which clogs wicking fibres.
